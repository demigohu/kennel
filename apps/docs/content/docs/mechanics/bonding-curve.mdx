A bonding curve is a price rule written in code. There is no order book and no market maker: you trade with the contract, and the price depends only on how much has been bought so far.

## The formula

Kennel uses the same model as pump.fun: a **constant product** with **virtual reserves**.

```
x = virtual NEAR + NEAR raised          (starts at 3.4125)
y = virtual tokens − tokens sold        (starts at 1,073,000,000)
x × y = k                               (stays constant on every trade)
price = x / y
```

A buy adds NEAR to `x`, so `y` must shrink to keep `x × y` constant. The tokens that leave `y` go to the buyer. The virtual reserves set the starting price and make the curve smooth from the first trade.

Early buyers get many more tokens per NEAR: the first 2 NEAR buy half of the curve. That is the reward for being early, and the reason the [snipe guard](/docs/mechanics/anti-snipe) keeps the launcher's family out of the first 2 minutes.

At graduation the pool opens with 200M tokens and about 9.75 NEAR, a price of about 0.0488 NEAR per 1M tokens. That is within 0.8% of the last curve price, so graduation doesn't jump the price.

## Fees

Every curve trade pays 1% of its NEAR amount:

<Diagram kind="Money flow" title="Where a 1 NEAR buy goes" chart={`

  F -- "0.99 NEAR" --> C["Curve reserve<br/>buys tokens"]
  F -- "0.005 NEAR" --> P["Creator pup budget<br/>only its handler withdraws"]
  F -- "0.005 NEAR" --> T["Protocol treasury<br/>kennel.testnet"]
  C --> W["Tokens land in<br/>the buyer's wallet"]
`} />

`withdraw_protocol_fees` is permissionless and always pays the treasury fixed at init.

## Slippage

Every buy and sell takes an optional minimum (`min_tokens_out` / `min_near_out`). If another trade lands first and moves the price past it, yours fails with `slippage` and nothing changes. The web app sets it from your max slippage; the CLI uses `--min-out`.
